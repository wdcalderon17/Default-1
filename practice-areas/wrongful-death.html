When negligence takes a life, Calderon Legal Group fights for the family left behind — pursuing the justice and financial security your loved one would have wanted for you.

Compassionate & Aggressive Wrongful Death Representation

Losing a loved one to someone else's carelessness is one of the most painful experiences a family can endure. In the midst of grief, navigating the legal system can feel overwhelming. Calderon Legal Group is here to shoulder that burden — allowing you to grieve and heal while we fight for the accountability and compensation your family deserves.

Texas wrongful death law gives surviving family members the right to pursue compensation when a death is caused by another person's negligence, recklessness, or intentional act.

Who Can File a Wrongful Death Claim in Texas?

Under the Texas Wrongful Death Act (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 71.001 et seq.), the following individuals may bring a wrongful death claim:

  • Surviving spouse
  • Children (including adopted children)
  • Parents

If no family member files within three months of the death, the executor or administrator of the estate may file on behalf of the beneficiaries.

Common Causes of Wrongful Death We Handle

  • Fatal car and truck accidents on I-45, I-10, and US-290
  • Fatal motorcycle crashes
  • Fatal workplace accidents and construction site deaths
  • Fatal slip and fall incidents
  • Medical malpractice and hospital negligence
  • Defective products and dangerous goods
  • Drunk driving fatalities
  • Pedestrian and bicycle fatalities

Damages Available in Texas Wrongful Death Cases

A wrongful death claim in Texas can compensate surviving family members for:

  • Loss of financial support and future earnings
  • Loss of care, maintenance, services, and advice
  • Loss of companionship and society
  • Mental anguish and grief suffered by survivors
  • Medical expenses incurred before death
  • Funeral and burial expenses (survival action)
  • Punitive damages (if gross negligence is proven)

Texas Wrongful Death Statute of Limitations

In Texas, most wrongful death claims must be filed within two years of the date of death. Waiting too long can permanently bar your family's right to compensation. Contact us as soon as possible to protect your legal rights.

Frequently Asked Questions

Texas's proportionate fault rules apply in wrongful death cases. Your family can still recover as long as the deceased was not more than 50% responsible. The recovery is reduced by the decedent's percentage of fault. Defense attorneys routinely try to inflate this percentage — our attorneys fight back with evidence.
A wrongful death claim compensates survivors for their own losses (income, companionship, etc.). A survival action is brought on behalf of the deceased's estate and recovers for what the decedent suffered before death — like medical bills and pain and suffering in the period between the injury and death. Both may be pursued simultaneously in Texas.
Most wrongful death cases settle before trial. However, we prepare every case as if it will go to trial, which significantly strengthens our negotiating position. If the defendant refuses to offer fair compensation, we are fully prepared to present your family's case to a Texas jury.

Houston Uninsured & Underinsured Motorist Attorneys

Texas has one of the highest rates of uninsured drivers in the nation. If you were hit by an uninsured or underinsured driver, our attorneys fight to make sure you still get the compensation you deserve — even if that means fighting your own insurance company.

Uninsured vs. Underinsured Motorist Coverage

Uninsured motorist (UM) and underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage are two types of protection you can purchase as part of your own auto insurance policy. Though related, they address different situations:

  • Uninsured motorist (UM) coverage — protects you when the at-fault driver carries no auto liability insurance at all, or when you are the victim of a hit-and-run and the at-fault driver cannot be identified
  • Underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage — protects you when the at-fault driver has liability insurance, but their policy limits are insufficient to cover the full extent of your injuries and damages

Texas consistently ranks among the states with the highest percentage of uninsured drivers — approximately 1 in 5 Texas drivers (roughly 20%) carries no auto insurance at all. On Houston's crowded freeways and surface streets, the odds of being hit by an uninsured motorist are significant. UM/UIM coverage can be the difference between full recovery and financial hardship after a serious accident.

When Does UM/UIM Coverage Apply?

  • Hit by an uninsured driver — the at-fault driver has no auto liability insurance; your UM coverage steps in to compensate you
  • Hit-and-run accident — the at-fault driver flees the scene and cannot be identified; UM coverage typically applies when there is physical contact between the vehicles (Texas requires this in most cases) or when there is corroborating evidence
  • At-fault driver's coverage is insufficient — the other driver is insured but their policy limits are too low to cover your medical bills, lost wages, and other damages; your UIM coverage makes up the gap

Why Your Own Insurance Company May Fight Your Claim

Many accident victims are surprised to learn that their own insurance company — not just the other driver's insurer — can be an adversary after an accident. When you file a UM/UIM claim, your insurer is essentially in the position of the at-fault driver's insurance company. They have a financial incentive to minimize what they pay you.

Common bad faith tactics used by insurers in UM/UIM claims include:

  • Disputing the severity of your injuries or claiming they are pre-existing
  • Requesting excessive documentation and delays to pressure you into accepting a low settlement
  • Arguing that you bear some fault for the accident to reduce your recovery
  • Making unreasonably low initial settlement offers
  • Failing to investigate your claim promptly as required by the Texas Prompt Payment of Claims Act

Calderon Legal Group represents UM/UIM claimants against their own insurers. We know how to hold insurance companies accountable to their contractual obligations — and to Texas insurance law.

Damages You Can Recover in a UM/UIM Claim

A successful UM/UIM claim can recover the same categories of damages as any personal injury claim, including:

  • Medical expenses — all past and future medical treatment costs related to your injuries
  • Lost wages — income you were unable to earn during your recovery
  • Lost earning capacity — reduced ability to earn income in the future due to your injuries
  • Pain and suffering — physical pain and emotional distress
  • Property damage — vehicle repair or replacement costs (subject to your policy terms)
  • Future medical care — ongoing treatment, therapy, and care needs

Your recovery is limited to your UM/UIM policy limits, which is why having adequate coverage is important. Our attorneys help you identify all available coverage — including policies covering other household members — to maximize your recovery.

Texas UM/UIM Law

Under the Texas Insurance Code, auto insurers are required to offer UM/UIM coverage to every policyholder. However, you may have waived this coverage — but any waiver must be made in writing and signed by the named insured. If you do not recall waiving UM/UIM coverage, or if you are unsure whether you have it, a written waiver may not be on file. We can help you determine what coverage you have.

Texas generally does not allow "stacking" of UM/UIM coverage (applying multiple policies for the same accident) unless the policies explicitly allow it. Our attorneys review all potentially applicable policies to find every source of coverage available to you.

In addition to compensatory damages, Texas law allows claims for bad faith against insurers who unreasonably deny or delay UM/UIM claims, potentially entitling you to additional damages and attorney's fees under the Texas Insurance Code and the DTPA.

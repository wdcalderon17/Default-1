Houston Birth Injury Attorneys

When medical negligence causes harm to a newborn or mother during childbirth, the consequences can last a lifetime. Our birth injury lawyers fight to hold negligent healthcare providers accountable.

What Is a Birth Injury?

A birth injury is physical harm suffered by a newborn or mother during pregnancy, labor, or delivery. Critically, birth injuries are distinct from birth defects. Birth defects are typically caused by genetic or developmental factors that occur before or during pregnancy and are not the result of anyone's fault. Birth injuries, by contrast, are caused by medical errors or negligent care — they are preventable.

When a doctor, nurse, midwife, or hospital fails to meet the accepted standard of care during childbirth, and that failure causes harm to the baby or mother, the family may have a valid birth injury claim. Houston is home to world-class medical institutions, including the Texas Medical Center — but even the best facilities employ providers who make life-altering mistakes. Calderon Legal Group helps Houston-area families hold those providers accountable.

Common Types of Birth Injuries We Handle

  • Cerebral palsy — brain damage caused by oxygen deprivation or trauma during delivery, resulting in lifelong movement and coordination disorders
  • Erb's palsy / brachial plexus injury — damage to the network of nerves controlling the arm and hand, often caused by excessive pulling during delivery
  • Hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE) — brain damage from insufficient oxygen supply to the baby's brain during labor or delivery
  • Skull fractures — caused by improper use of forceps, vacuum extractors, or excessive force during delivery
  • Brain damage from oxygen deprivation — resulting from prolonged labor, umbilical cord complications, or delayed emergency C-section
  • Shoulder dystocia injuries — when the baby's shoulder becomes stuck during delivery and is handled improperly, causing nerve damage or fractures
  • Facial nerve injuries — from forceps use or prolonged difficult labor
  • Spinal cord injuries — caused by excessive traction or improper positioning during delivery

Medical Errors That Cause Birth Injuries

Birth injuries are often the result of one or more of the following medical errors:

  • Failure to monitor fetal distress — ignoring or misreading signs of fetal heart rate abnormalities that signal oxygen deprivation
  • Delayed C-section — failing to order an emergency cesarean section when complications indicate it is necessary
  • Improper use of forceps or vacuum extractors — applying excessive force or using these instruments incorrectly
  • Medication errors — administering incorrect drugs or dosages to the mother, including Pitocin overdose causing uterine hyperstimulation
  • Failure to diagnose and treat maternal conditions — preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, infections, or placental problems that go unmanaged
  • Inadequate response to prolapsed umbilical cord — failure to act quickly when the cord compresses and cuts off oxygen to the baby
  • Premature discharge of the mother — sending the mother home before complications are resolved

Compensation for Birth Injury Families

The cost of caring for a child with a serious birth injury can reach millions of dollars over a lifetime. Families may be entitled to compensation covering:

  • Lifetime medical care — hospitalization, surgeries, specialist visits, medications, and ongoing therapies
  • Special education and developmental services — educational support, assistive technology, and developmental programs
  • Physical, occupational, and speech therapy costs — often needed for years or decades
  • Lost earning capacity for the child — compensation for the child's reduced ability to earn income as an adult
  • Pain and suffering — physical pain and emotional distress suffered by the child
  • Parental emotional distress — the profound psychological impact on parents who must care for a seriously injured child
  • Home care and assistive equipment — wheelchairs, home modifications, and professional in-home caregivers

Texas Birth Injury Statute of Limitations

Timing is critical in birth injury cases. Texas medical malpractice claims are generally subject to a two-year statute of limitations from the date the injury occurred or was discovered. However, special rules apply for minor children. In Texas, the statute of limitations for minors is typically tolled until the child reaches age 12, but medical malpractice claims must generally be filed within 10 years of the act or omission that caused the injury — a critical outer limit known as the statute of repose (Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 74.251).

These rules are complex and fact-specific. Do not assume you have unlimited time to act. Contact Calderon Legal Group as soon as possible to have your case evaluated and ensure your family's rights are protected.

Birth Injury FAQ

Determining whether a birth injury was caused by medical negligence requires a thorough review of medical records by qualified experts. The process begins with obtaining complete prenatal, labor, and delivery records, as well as the newborn's hospital records. Our attorneys work with board-certified OB/GYN physicians, neonatologists, and other specialists who review these records to identify deviations from the accepted standard of care. If the evidence suggests negligence, we move forward with the claim. This expert review process is required under Texas law before a medical malpractice suit can be filed.

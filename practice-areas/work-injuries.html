Beyond Workers' Comp: Third-Party Workplace Injury Claims

If you were injured on the job in Texas, you may be entitled to workers' compensation benefits from your employer. However, workers' comp only provides limited benefits — it doesn't cover pain and suffering, and it severely limits lost wage recovery.

Many workplace accidents also involve third-party liability — meaning a party other than your employer caused or contributed to your injury. When that's the case, you may be able to file a separate personal injury lawsuit that can recover full damages, including pain and suffering, that workers' comp does not allow.

Common Third-Party Workplace Injury Scenarios in Texas

  • Construction site accidents — caused by a subcontractor, property owner, or equipment manufacturer
  • Delivery and transportation accidents — a crash caused by another driver while you were working
  • Defective equipment or machinery — manufacturer liability claims
  • Oilfield and petrochemical plant accidents — involving contractors and product liability
  • Slip and fall on a client's property — the property owner may be liable
  • Toxic exposure — from third-party chemicals or negligent handling

Common Workplace Injuries We Handle

  • Falls from heights (ladders, scaffolding, roofs)
  • Struck-by accidents (falling objects, machinery)
  • Caught-in/between accidents
  • Electrical and electrocution injuries
  • Burn injuries from chemicals, fires, or explosions
  • Forklift and heavy equipment accidents
  • Repetitive stress and overexertion injuries
  • Chemical and toxin exposure

Important: Texas Non-Subscriber Employers

Texas is the only state that does not require employers to carry workers' compensation insurance. If your employer "opted out" of the workers' comp system (a "non-subscriber"), you have the right to sue them directly in civil court for your injuries — and the employer cannot use most common defenses like contributory negligence.

This can significantly increase your recovery. Our attorneys will immediately determine whether your employer is a subscriber or non-subscriber and advise you on the best legal strategy.

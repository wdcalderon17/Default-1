Texas Theft Crime Classifications

In Texas, theft offenses are classified by the value of the property stolen:

  • Class C Misdemeanor: Value under $100 — up to $500 fine
  • Class B Misdemeanor: $100–$750 — up to 180 days jail, $2,000 fine
  • Class A Misdemeanor: $750–$2,500 — up to 1 year jail, $4,000 fine
  • State Jail Felony: $2,500–$30,000 — 180 days–2 years state jail, $10,000 fine
  • 3rd Degree Felony: $30,000–$150,000 — 2–10 years prison, $10,000 fine
  • 2nd Degree Felony: $150,000–$300,000 — 2–20 years prison, $10,000 fine
  • 1st Degree Felony: Over $300,000 — 5–99 years prison, $10,000 fine

Prior theft convictions can elevate the charge level, turning what would be a misdemeanor into a felony.

Types of Theft Cases We Defend

  • Shoplifting / Retail Theft — the most common theft charge; often Class B or C misdemeanors
  • Larceny — taking someone else's property without permission
  • Robbery — theft involving use or threat of force; classified as a 2nd degree felony in Texas
  • Aggravated Robbery — robbery with a deadly weapon or against an elderly/disabled person; 1st degree felony
  • Theft by Fraud / Check Fraud — deceptive schemes to obtain property or services
  • Burglary of a Vehicle — breaking into a vehicle to commit theft; Class A misdemeanor or state jail felony
  • Burglary of a Habitation or Building — entering a structure with intent to commit theft or felony; 2nd degree felony or higher

Potential Consequences of a Theft Conviction

  • Jail or prison time
  • Fines and court costs
  • Civil restitution demands from retailers
  • Permanent criminal record affecting employment and housing
  • Immigration consequences — theft is a crime of moral turpitude that can affect visa/green card status
  • Loss of professional licenses

Defenses to Theft Charges

As a former prosecutor, Attorney Calderon knows exactly what the State must prove — and where its case may be weak:

  • Lack of intent: The State must prove intentional theft; mistakes and accidents are not crimes
  • Claim of right: You genuinely believed the property was yours
  • Insufficient evidence: Store video, witness testimony, or other evidence may be weak or missing
  • Illegal search and seizure: Evidence obtained in violation of the 4th Amendment may be suppressed
  • Entrapment: Law enforcement induced you to commit a crime you would not otherwise have committed

Can a Theft Charge Be Expunged in Texas?

Possibly. If you receive deferred adjudication and successfully complete your probation, you may be eligible for a non-disclosure order — which seals your record from most public view. If your case was dismissed or you were acquitted, you may qualify for full expunction. Attorney Calderon evaluates each client's eligibility and pursues the best available record-clearing option.

Houston Rideshare Accident Lawyers (Uber & Lyft)

Rideshare accidents involving Uber and Lyft create complex insurance coverage questions. Our Houston attorneys know exactly how to pursue maximum compensation from all available policies.

Why Rideshare Accident Claims Are Different

When you're injured in a rideshare accident in Houston, the path to compensation is far more complicated than a standard car accident. Uber and Lyft accidents involve multiple overlapping insurance policies — the driver's personal auto insurance and Uber's or Lyft's corporate coverage — and which policy applies depends on exactly what the driver was doing at the moment of the crash.

Both Uber and Lyft classify their drivers as independent contractors rather than employees, which allows them to distance themselves from liability. They use this classification to argue their drivers' actions are not the company's responsibility. An experienced rideshare accident attorney knows how to navigate these arguments and identify every available source of compensation for your injuries.

Uber & Lyft Insurance Coverage in Texas

Understanding Uber and Lyft's insurance coverage requires knowing what "phase" the driver was in at the time of the accident:

  • Phase 1 — App offline (driver not logged in): The driver's personal auto insurance applies exclusively. Uber and Lyft provide no coverage. If the driver has inadequate personal coverage, your recovery options may be limited.
  • Phase 2 — App on, waiting for a ride request: Uber and Lyft provide contingent liability coverage of $50,000 per person / $100,000 per accident for bodily injury and $25,000 for property damage — but only if the driver's personal insurance does not apply or is insufficient.
  • Phase 3 — Active ride (driver has accepted a trip or passenger is in the vehicle): Uber and Lyft provide their full $1,000,000 liability policy, plus uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage and contingent comprehensive/collision coverage. This is the most protective phase for injury victims.

Determining which phase applied requires obtaining records from Uber or Lyft showing the driver's app status at the time of the crash. Our attorneys know how to compel this information and use it to build your claim.

Common Types of Rideshare Accident Claims

  • Passenger injured in an Uber or Lyft — you were a paying passenger when the rideshare driver caused or was involved in a crash
  • Pedestrian struck by a rideshare driver — an Uber or Lyft driver hit you while you were walking, cycling, or standing near the roadway
  • Rideshare driver injured in an accident — the rideshare driver was hurt in a crash caused by another driver; coverage depends on app phase and fault
  • Another driver hit by a rideshare driver — you were in your own vehicle when an Uber or Lyft driver caused a collision

Houston Rideshare Accident Hot Spots

Rideshare pickups and drop-offs are concentrated in certain high-traffic areas where accident risk is elevated:

  • Downtown Houston — heavy rideshare activity around bars, restaurants, and entertainment venues, especially late nights and weekends
  • Midtown and Montrose — one of Houston's busiest nightlife and dining corridors
  • The Woodlands — growing rideshare demand around the Waterway and entertainment district
  • George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IAH) and William P. Hobby Airport (HOU) — designated rideshare pickup/dropoff zones with congested traffic patterns
  • Texas Medical Center — heavy rideshare traffic from patients, visitors, and healthcare workers
  • I-45 / I-610 interchange and surrounding freeways — high-speed rideshare trips on Houston's busiest corridors

What to Do After a Rideshare Accident

  • Call 911 — ensure police respond and an accident report is created
  • Take screenshots of the app — immediately capture screenshots showing your trip details, driver information, and trip status before closing the app
  • Photograph the scene — document vehicle damage, road conditions, traffic signals, and any visible injuries
  • Get all insurance information — collect the driver's personal insurance card and note the vehicle's license plate
  • Seek immediate medical attention — even if you feel fine; some injuries appear later, and medical records are critical to your claim
  • Contact an attorney before speaking to insurance — both the rideshare company's insurer and the driver's personal insurer will contact you; do not give recorded statements without legal advice

Rideshare Accident FAQ

Generally, lawsuits in rideshare accidents are brought against the driver, not Uber or Lyft directly, because both companies classify their drivers as independent contractors rather than employees. However, when the driver had the app open and accepted a trip, Uber's or Lyft's $1,000,000 insurance policy covers the accident. In practice, this means you are pursuing compensation through Uber's or Lyft's insurance — even if the company itself is not named as a defendant. There are some situations where direct liability claims against the company may be viable; our attorneys evaluate every angle of your specific case.
If another driver caused the accident, you can pursue compensation from that driver's liability insurance. If that driver is uninsured or underinsured and does not have enough coverage to fully compensate you, you may also be able to pursue a claim under Uber's or Lyft's uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage, which applies during active trips. As a passenger, you have strong rights to compensation regardless of who caused the accident — you were not at fault as a rider.
Texas has a two-year statute of limitations for personal injury claims, including rideshare accidents. However, acting quickly is critical for reasons beyond the legal deadline. App trip records, GPS data, and driver status records are held by Uber and Lyft and may be more difficult to obtain as time passes. Witness memories fade. Security camera footage gets overwritten. Preserving this evidence early significantly strengthens your case. Contact an attorney as soon as possible after a rideshare accident.

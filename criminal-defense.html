Criminal Defense Cases We Handle

DWI / DUI Defense

Texas has some of the strictest DWI (Driving While Intoxicated) laws in the country. A first-offense DWI is a Class B misdemeanor, but convictions can result in jail time, fines up to $2,000 (plus surcharges), license suspension, and required installation of an ignition interlock device. Subsequent offenses or DWI with a child passenger elevate the charge to a felony.

One critical and often-overlooked aspect of a Texas DWI arrest is the Administrative License Revocation (ALR) hearing. You have only 15 days from the date of your arrest to request an ALR hearing or your license will be automatically suspended. Attorney Calderon handles both the criminal DWI case and the ALR hearing to give you the best chance of keeping your driving privileges.

We examine every aspect of your DWI case: the validity of the traffic stop, whether field sobriety tests were properly administered, the calibration records of breathalyzer equipment, and whether blood draw procedures followed proper protocols.

Assault & Family Violence

Assault charges in Texas range from a Class C misdemeanor (threat of bodily injury) to a first-degree felony (aggravated assault with a deadly weapon causing serious bodily injury). The level of the charge depends on the type of contact, the severity of injury, and whether certain protected relationships or weapons were involved.

Family violence assault charges carry additional consequences: a conviction creates a permanent family violence finding on your record that can never be expunged, affects your right to possess firearms under federal law, and can have devastating effects on child custody proceedings. If a protective order has been issued against you, violating it creates a separate criminal offense. Calderon Legal Group handles both the criminal defense and advises on protective order proceedings.

Drug Possession

Drug possession charges in Texas are governed by the Texas Controlled Substances Act, which categorizes controlled substances into penalty groups. The severity of the charge depends on the type of substance (Penalty Group 1 through 4), the quantity, and whether the facts support an intent-to-deliver enhancement. For example, simple possession of a small amount of marijuana may be a Class B misdemeanor, while possession of more than 400 grams of a Penalty Group 1 substance (such as heroin or methamphetamine) carries a minimum 10-year prison sentence.

Mr. Calderon scrutinizes every drug possession case for Fourth Amendment violations — illegal searches and seizures that may result in suppression of evidence and dismissal of charges.

Theft Crimes

Theft charges in Texas range from a Class C misdemeanor for theft of property valued under $100 all the way to a first-degree felony for theft of $300,000 or more. The consequences — including a permanent criminal record that affects employment, housing, and immigration status — can follow you for life.

The Criminal Defense Process in Texas

Understanding what to expect can reduce anxiety and help you make informed decisions. Here is a general overview of the Texas criminal process:

  1. Arrest & Booking — You are arrested, booked, and a bond is set. Contact an attorney as soon as possible — ideally before speaking to law enforcement.
  2. Arraignment — You appear before a judge, are formally informed of the charges, and enter a plea. Your attorney can often appear on your behalf for misdemeanor arraignments.
  3. Discovery — Your attorney obtains all evidence the prosecution intends to use: police reports, body camera footage, lab results, witness statements. This is where case weaknesses are identified.
  4. Pretrial Motions — Your attorney may file motions to suppress illegally obtained evidence, dismiss charges for lack of probable cause, or challenge the constitutionality of the stop or arrest.
  5. Plea Negotiations — In many cases, the best outcome is a negotiated plea to a lesser charge, deferred adjudication, or pretrial diversion. An attorney who knows the DA's office and the strength of the evidence is invaluable here.
  6. Trial — If no acceptable resolution is reached, your case goes to trial before a judge or jury. William Calderon is an experienced trial attorney with a background in prosecuting complex jury trials.

What to Do Immediately After a Car Accident in Houston

The steps you take in the first 24 hours after a crash can make or break your personal injury claim. Here's what Houston injury attorneys recommend.

Houston sees tens of thousands of car accidents every year. Whether you've been in a fender bender on the Katy Freeway or a serious collision on I-45, what you do in the moments and hours following the crash can have a profound impact on your health — and on your ability to recover compensation through a personal injury claim.

As personal injury attorneys who have represented hundreds of Houston accident victims, we've seen how the right steps can maximize a recovery and how the wrong ones can devastate a claim. Here's a step-by-step guide to protecting yourself after a car accident in Houston.

Step 1: Check for Injuries — Call 911

Your safety is the top priority. Check yourself and your passengers for injuries. If anyone is hurt — or even if you're not sure — call 911 immediately. In Houston, a police report is a critical piece of evidence in your personal injury case. Officers will document the scene, record statements, and often assign fault in their report.

Do not move injured people unless they are in immediate danger (e.g., from fire or oncoming traffic). Moving an injured person can worsen spinal or neck injuries.

Step 2: Move to Safety if Possible

If the vehicles are driveable and no one appears seriously injured, move them out of traffic to the shoulder or a parking lot to prevent secondary accidents. Turn on hazard lights and set up warning triangles or flares if available. Houston's highways — especially I-10 and I-45 — carry fast-moving traffic where secondary accidents are common.

Step 3: Document the Scene

This is critical for your claim. Use your smartphone to capture:

  • All vehicles involved — from multiple angles, including damage and license plates
  • The overall accident scene — traffic signals, road markings, skid marks
  • Any visible injuries to yourself or your passengers
  • Weather and road conditions
  • Nearby surveillance cameras or businesses that might have footage

Take more photos than you think you need. It's impossible to take too many.

Step 4: Exchange Information — But Don't Admit Fault

Collect the following from all other drivers:

  • Full name, address, phone number
  • Driver's license number
  • Vehicle registration and license plate
  • Insurance company name and policy number

Important: Be polite, but do not admit fault, apologize, or speculate about what happened. Even saying "I'm sorry" can be used against you by insurance companies. Just stick to the facts when speaking with police.

Step 5: Get Witness Information

Eyewitnesses can be invaluable in disputed liability cases. If bystanders or other drivers stopped to observe, politely ask for their name and phone number before they leave the scene. Witnesses fade away quickly.

Step 6: Seek Medical Attention — Even if You Feel Fine

This is one of the most critical steps, and one that many accident victims skip — to their detriment. Many serious injuries — including whiplash, traumatic brain injury (TBI), internal bleeding, and spinal injuries — do not produce immediate symptoms. Adrenaline masks pain.

Seeing a doctor promptly after an accident creates a medical record linking your injuries to the crash. If you wait days or weeks, insurance companies will argue your injuries were caused by something else. Visit an emergency room, urgent care, or your primary care physician as soon as possible — ideally the same day.

Step 7: Report the Accident to Your Insurance — But Don't Give a Recorded Statement

You are required to report an accident to your own insurance company. However, you are not required to give a recorded statement to the at-fault driver's insurance company. In fact, you should not do so before consulting with an attorney. Insurance adjusters are trained to ask questions that can minimize your claim.

Step 8: Contact a Houston Personal Injury Attorney

Before accepting any settlement offer — or even before speaking further with insurance companies — consult with a personal injury attorney. This consultation is free, and it can be the difference between receiving fair compensation and leaving thousands of dollars on the table.

What Not to Do After a Houston Car Accident

  • Don't leave the scene (it's a criminal offense in Texas)
  • Don't post about the accident on social media — insurers monitor this
  • Don't accept a quick settlement before understanding your full injuries and damages
  • Don't sign any releases without an attorney reviewing them
  • Don't delay medical treatment — gaps in treatment hurt your claim

Key Texas Laws to Know

  • Two-year statute of limitations — you have two years from the accident date to file a personal injury lawsuit in Texas.
  • Modified comparative fault — if you are 50% or less at fault, you can still recover; your damages are reduced by your percentage of fault.
  • Minimum insurance requirements — Texas requires 30/60/25 liability coverage, but many drivers are underinsured or uninsured. Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage matters.

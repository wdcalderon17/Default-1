Life After a Car Accident in College Station

College Station and Bryan are growing fast — and so are their roads. Here's what injury victims in Brazos County need to know about protecting their health and their legal rights after a crash.

College Station and Bryan together form a thriving metropolitan area at the heart of Brazos County — home to Texas A&M University and nearly 250,000 residents. The region's rapid population growth has brought with it heavier traffic, more congested intersections, and a steady increase in car accidents. Whether you were rear-ended on Harvey Mitchell Pkwy, T-boned at a Texas Ave intersection, clipped on University Dr near campus, or struck on State Highway 6, the aftermath of a crash can be overwhelming.

Dealing with injuries, missed work, mounting medical bills, and pushy insurance adjusters all at once is a lot — especially if you've never been through it before. This guide walks you through what to do, what to avoid, and how an experienced personal injury attorney can help you recover the compensation you deserve.

Immediate Steps After a Crash in Brazos County

The actions you take in the minutes and hours following a collision can have a lasting impact on both your health and any future legal claim. Here's what to do:

  • Call 911. Even if the crash seems minor, a police report from the College Station Police Department or Brazos County Sheriff's Office creates an official record that is invaluable for insurance claims and litigation.
  • Document the scene. Use your phone to photograph all vehicles from multiple angles, visible injuries, road conditions, traffic signals, skid marks, and nearby signage. Note the location — whether it's an SH-6 overpass or a Campus Drive intersection — as local context matters.
  • Seek medical care even if you feel fine. Adrenaline and shock often mask injury symptoms. Many serious conditions — including whiplash, spinal injury, and traumatic brain injury — may not become apparent until hours or days after the crash. Go to an emergency room, urgent care, or your doctor the same day.
  • Notify your insurance company. You are required to report the accident, but keep your statement brief and factual.
  • Do NOT give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer. Insurance adjusters are trained to ask leading questions that minimize your claim. Politely decline until you've spoken with an attorney.

Medical Care in College Station After an Accident

Prompt medical attention is not just about your health — it is also one of the most important steps you can take to protect your personal injury claim. When you see a doctor immediately after a crash, the medical records establish a clear causal link between the accident and your injuries. Without that documentation, insurance companies will argue that your injuries were pre-existing or caused by something unrelated.

Do not minimize your symptoms when speaking with medical providers. Pain in your neck, back, or head, dizziness, difficulty concentrating, or emotional distress are all legitimate injuries that should be reported and documented. Keep copies of all medical records, bills, prescription receipts, and any documentation of missed work. These records form the foundation of your damages calculation.

Delayed treatment — even a gap of a few days — gives insurers a powerful argument to reduce or deny your claim. If College Station or Bryan emergency rooms are busy, an urgent care clinic is still far better than waiting.

Dealing with Insurance in Texas

Texas is an at-fault state, meaning the driver who caused the accident (and their insurance company) is responsible for compensating the victims. However, navigating that process is rarely straightforward.

Texas follows a modified comparative negligence rule (also called proportionate responsibility). As long as you are 50% or less at fault for the accident, you can still recover damages — but your award will be reduced by your percentage of fault. This is why insurance companies often try to assign partial blame to you: it directly reduces what they must pay.

Never accept the first settlement offer from an insurance company without consulting an attorney. Initial offers are almost always lower than the true value of your claim. Insurers count on injured people to accept quick payouts before they understand the full extent of their injuries and future medical needs. Once you sign a release, you cannot go back for more — even if your condition worsens.

The Role of a Local Personal Injury Attorney

Hiring an attorney who knows Brazos County makes a measurable difference. Local counsel understands the tendencies of Brazos County district court judges, the procedures of the 85th and 272nd District Courts, and the way College Station Police and the Brazos County Sheriff's Office document crash scenes. This knowledge shapes how your case is prepared and presented.

SH-6 is one of the most accident-prone corridors in the region, seeing a disproportionate share of serious collisions involving commercial trucks, distracted drivers, and out-of-county motorists unfamiliar with local interchange patterns. University Drive near the Texas A&M campus is another chronic trouble spot, particularly during football season and the academic year. A local attorney will have handled cases arising from these exact locations and understand the evidence that matters most.

Beyond courtroom familiarity, a personal injury attorney handles all communications with insurance companies on your behalf, works to secure evidence before it disappears, retains medical and accident reconstruction experts when needed, and negotiates aggressively for a fair settlement.

How Long Do You Have to File in Texas?

Texas imposes a two-year statute of limitations on personal injury claims arising from car accidents. Under Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 16.003, you must file your lawsuit within two years of the date of the accident — or you lose your right to sue entirely, regardless of how strong your case is.

There are limited exceptions to this rule:

  • Minors: If the injured person was under 18 at the time of the accident, the limitations period is generally tolled until they turn 18, giving them until age 20 to file.
  • Government entities: If a government vehicle or entity was involved, you may need to file a formal notice of claim within as little as six months. These deadlines are even stricter, so acting quickly is critical.

Two years may sound like a long time, but evidence degrades, witnesses become harder to locate, and critical records may be destroyed. The sooner you speak with an attorney, the stronger your case will be.
